---

## Who Jolene is (her real reality)
- She sells **Buddy** (see PRODUCT-BUDDY.md). She leads sales at Dev Difference.
- Her job is **NOT research.** Her job is **get a founder to reply and take a meeting.**
- She's measured on **booked meetings**, not dossiers read.
- She's doing **volume** — lots of outreach. Every hour spent figuring out *who to
  hit and what to say* is an hour NOT spent actually hitting them.
- **Decision fatigue and the blank message box are her real enemies**, not lack of data.

---

## What Jolene wants from a sales agent (priority order)

### 1. "Tell me who to hit today, and why — don't make me decide."
- Not a database of 500 companies to sift. A **short, judged list**: *"These 8.
  Start here. This one's hottest — they opened 30 roles this week."*
- The agent has **already done the judging.** A queue she has to *evaluate* is just
  more work. Decision fatigue is the enemy.

### 2. "Write the words for me. Good words. That I'd actually send."
- The blank message box is the slowest part of her day.
- Pitch **already written** — personalized, specific, referencing exactly why *this*
  founder is drowning now ("saw you're hiring 12 engineers — screening that flood is
  brutal; Buddy interviews all of them for you").
- NOT a `[FIRST_NAME]` template. Words she can **glance-tweak-and-send, or just send.**

### 3. "Give me the warm way in, not just an email address."
- Cold is hard. She wants: **the right person + a hook that isn't creepy + why now.**
- The **entry point** (right person + why it's warm) is the gold — it's what lifts
  reply rate.

### 4. "Don't let me look stupid or spam."
- Keep out: already-said-no, already-emailed, clearly-not-a-fit (not a startup, not
  hiring, 5000 employees).
- She trusts the agent MORE every time it **doesn't** waste her time.

### 5. "Keep the well full without me thinking about it."
- Never hit the bottom of the list and go "now what?"
- Fresh, hot leads just **appear.** (This is the harvester — but from her seat she
  doesn't care how. Never empty, never stale.)

### 6. "Close the loop — did it work?"
- Which messages got replies? Which hook is landing? **Quietly learn** so tomorrow's
  picks and pitches are better. She doesn't want to manage this; it should just get
  smarter.

---

## The thing underneath all six
Jolene doesn't want a **tool**. She wants **a teammate who did the homework.**

**The dream:** she opens it each morning and it says —
> *"Here are today's 8. I already wrote your openers. Start with Winston — he's the
> hottest and here's why."*

She spends her energy on the **human part** (the actual conversation), not the
grind (who / what / is-it-worth-it).

---

## How this reshapes Scout
- Scout today is a beautiful **dossier**. But Jolene doesn't want to *read* — she
  wants to be **told and armed.**
- The agent's job is NOT "research a company well." It's:
  > **Reduce Jolene's daily decision to: hit send or skip.**
- Everything — harvester, scoring, pitch-writing, loop-closing — serves ONE outcome:
  **fewer decisions, better sends, more replies (→ booked meetings).**

## The one-line test for every future decision
> **"Does this move Jolene closer to *just hit send*, or does it add a decision /
> a read / a step?"** If it adds friction, it's wrong — no matter how cool.
